# Taste: what real reviews confirmed

These are the default rules. They come from one product owner's reviews of two products
designed with this skill. If you are working for someone else, ask what they like and keep a
copy of this file with their answers; their taste wins.

## Liked — keep doing

- **One signature object in the hero that shows the mechanism with real data**, interactive
  (drag to turn, arrow keys to step), with things happening on it.
- **Drawn characters**: seeded faces that blink and nod, one per person. Never photos.
- **Colour as identity**: whole sections in one colour; "life and joy".
- **Icons that act out the sentence beside them**, not generic symbols.
- **A step-by-step animated explanation** of how the product works.
- **One character acting out each of the visitor's problems**, with feeling, ending on the
  turn to the product.
- **A small live detail by the headline** that says the headline again without words (for
  "Hundreds of applications. Not one reply.": a "typing…" bubble that appears, bounces and
  disappears without sending anything).
- A mascot used sparingly (empty states, sign-in), and a logo that tells the product's story.
- Copy that names the visitor's real frustration before it names a feature.

## Rejected — don't repeat

- **A globe / planet / world-map hero or logo.** "It's repeating too much, I see it in many
  designs." Not a globe, earth, dotted map or orbiting planet, ever.
- **Heavy or decorative display fonts**, and more than one type family.
- **Too many colours**: five section colours was too many; four was right.
- **Literal metaphor costumes** (shopfronts, awnings, newspaper mastheads) wrapped around the
  content.
- **Crowded pages**: too many objects per section.
- **Brown or muddy tiles in dark mode** (a light tint mixed into a dark ground).
- A dashboard that looks basic next to the homepage, or that breaks on a phone.
- **Drawn human faces as the cast** (FundVec, 2026-11-19): ink line faces, then cut-paper
  collage faces, were both rejected ("change them totally"). What worked: an object mascot from
  the product's own world (the logo come alive) and people shown as objects (postage stamps).
- **Two products that look alike.** The house approach is shared, the look must not be. This
  is why `worlds.json` and the direction step exist.

## Standing bans (from general frontend practice)

No label or eyebrow above a heading, no gradient text, no monospace as decoration, no grid of
identical cards as the page, no emoji as icons, no grey secondary text, no pure black ink,
no `opacity` as the hover state (lift and shadow instead).
