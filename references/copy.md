# Writing the page

Design gets a visitor to read; the words decide whether they stay. The order that worked:

1. **Headline: the visitor's situation, not the product.** "Hundreds of applications. Not one
   reply." beats "AI-powered job applications". Short, concrete, in their words.
2. **The problem section: go to the feeling.** List the real frustrations one by one, each a
   plain sentence followed by two or three lines of what it is like ("You refresh your inbox
   between interviews that never come"). Name the systems and people causing it (screening
   software, every hiring team wanting something different). Do not lecture the visitor or
   tell them they are doing it wrong; do not contradict what they know about their own market.
3. **The turn.** One line that takes the weight off ("That is the part we take off you."),
   then the product, as a sequence of what it does for them, in their order of the day.
4. **The mechanism, shown.** The hero object and the tour carry the proof; the copy points at
   them instead of repeating adjectives.
5. **Pricing in plain numbers**, with what each plan does per day or per month, derived from
   the same limits the code enforces.
6. **FAQ for the real doubts**: will it invent things about me, where does my data go, what
   happens when it is wrong, why no free plan.
7. **Close on the life they get back**, not the feature ("Get your evenings back.").

Rules: every number is real and comes from the product's data; no invented testimonials or
logos; no "revolutionary", "seamless", "unlock", "supercharge"; sentences a person would say
aloud; one idea per paragraph.
